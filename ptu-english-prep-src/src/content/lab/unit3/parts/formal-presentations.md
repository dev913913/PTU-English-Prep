# Formal Presentations

## What is a Formal Presentation?

A **formal presentation** is a planned and organised spoken presentation in which a speaker presents information, ideas, or a viewpoint to an audience for a specific purpose.

It may be used in a **classroom, seminar, meeting, interview, workplace, or academic event**.

A good formal presentation should be **clear, organised, audience-aware, and appropriately delivered**.

---

## Why Do We Give Presentations?

A presentation may be used to:

* Inform an audience.
* Explain a topic or process.
* Share ideas or findings.
* Persuade or influence an audience.
* Report the results of a project or activity.

---

## Basic Structure of a Presentation

A simple formal presentation can be divided into three main parts:

### 1. Opening and Introduction

Start politely and tell the audience what you are going to discuss.

**Example:**

> "Good morning everyone. Today I am going to talk about the importance of time management for college students."

A good opening may include:

* Greeting
* Topic
* Purpose
* Brief outline of the main points

### 2. Main Body

Explain the main points in a logical order.

Use:

* Short and clear points
* Examples
* Facts or simple evidence where appropriate
* Transitions between ideas

**Useful transitions:**

* "First, let us look at..."
* "The second point is..."
* "For example..."
* "Moving on to..."
* "Finally..."

### 3. Conclusion

Briefly remind the audience of the main message.

**Example:**

> "To conclude, good time management helps students reduce stress, meet deadlines, and make better use of their time. Thank you for listening."

Do not introduce a completely new major idea in the conclusion.

---

## How to Begin a Presentation

Avoid starting with:

> "My topic is..." and immediately reading a definition.

Instead, try to create interest.

**Question:**  
"How many of us have said, 'I will study tomorrow'?"

**Short fact:**  
"Most of us have more than 24 hours of work but only 24 hours in a day."

**Situation:**  
"Imagine having an examination tomorrow and three chapters still left to study."

Then introduce the topic.

## Delivery Skills

Good content is important, but **how you deliver it** also matters.

### Voice

Use your voice naturally.

* **Pitch:** A slightly higher pitch can show enthusiasm or excitement; a lower pitch can suit serious or calm points.
* **Volume:** Speak loudly enough for the audience to hear, but do not shout.
* **Rate:** Slow down when explaining a difficult or important idea. Avoid speaking so fast that words become unclear.
* **Pause:** Pause before or after an important point to give it emphasis.

### Body Language

* Maintain suitable eye contact.
* Stand or sit with an open and confident posture.
* Use natural hand gestures.
* Avoid constantly looking at the floor or screen.
* Avoid unnecessary movements that distract the audience.

### Language

Use language that is:

* Clear
* Simple
* Correct
* Appropriate for the audience and situation

Do not use difficult vocabulary merely to sound impressive.

---

## Using Visual Aids

Visual aids can support a presentation. Examples include:

* Slides
* Pictures
* Charts
* Diagrams
* Short videos

A visual aid should **support the speaker, not replace the speaker**.

Avoid putting long paragraphs on every slide. Use short points and explain them in your own words.

---

## Common Mistakes

### 1. Reading the Entire Presentation

Notes should support your memory. Do not keep your eyes fixed on the paper or screen.

### 2. Speaking Too Fast

Nervousness often increases speaking speed. Slow down and pause between ideas.

### 3. Overloading Slides

Too much text makes a slide difficult to read and encourages the speaker to read instead of explain.

### 4. Ignoring the Audience

A presentation is communication with people, not simply reading information aloud.

### 5. Weak Opening or Ending

The audience should know what the presentation is about and what the main takeaway is.

### 6. Poor Time Management

Practise so that the presentation fits the given time.

## Short Sample Presentation

### Topic: Importance of Communication Skills

> "Good morning everyone. Today I would like to talk about the importance of communication skills.
>
> Communication is a part of almost every area of our lives. First, good communication helps us express our ideas clearly. Second, it helps us understand other people and work with them effectively. Finally, communication skills are important in interviews, workplaces, and professional life.
>
> Therefore, communication is not only about speaking English. It is also about listening, understanding, choosing the right words, and responding appropriately.
>
> To conclude, strong communication skills can help us learn better, work better, and build better relationships. Thank you."

## Presentation Practice

### Task 1 — One-Minute Presentation

Choose one topic:

* Importance of English for college students
* Social media: useful or distracting?
* Why should students learn programming?
* Importance of time management
* My ideal workplace

Speak for **one minute**.

Try to include:

1. Opening
2. Two or three main points
3. Conclusion

### Task 2 — Delivery Challenge

Give the same presentation twice.

**First time:** focus on content.

**Second time:** deliberately improve your:

* Eye contact
* Rate of speech
* Volume
* Pauses
* Hand gestures

Compare the two performances.

---

## Quick Checklist

Before presenting, ask yourself:

- Do I know my main message?
- Is my presentation logically organised?
- Is my opening clear?
- Can the audience hear me?
- Am I speaking too fast?
- Am I making suitable eye contact?
- Are my slides or notes helping rather than distracting?
- Can I finish within the given time?

## Remember

**A good presentation is not a speech that is memorised perfectly. It is a message that is organised clearly and delivered naturally to an audience.**
