# Public Speaking

## What is Public Speaking?

**Public speaking** is the act of speaking to an audience in a planned or organised way.

It may be used for:

* Classroom speeches
* Seminars
* Presentations
* Meetings
* Awareness programmes
* Formal events

Public speaking does not mean speaking to hundreds of people only. A speech to a classroom is also public speaking.

---

## Basic Structure of a Speech

A short speech can follow three main parts:

### 1. Opening

Gain attention and introduce the topic.

You can begin with:

* A question
* A short story
* A surprising fact
* A relevant situation
* A clear statement

**Example:**

> "How many times have we checked our phones while studying?"

Then introduce the topic.

### 2. Body

Develop two or three main points.

Use examples, explanations, and transitions.

Useful transitions:

* "First..."
* "Another important point is..."
* "For example..."
* "However..."
* "Finally..."

### 3. Conclusion

Summarise the main message and end clearly.

> "To conclude, technology is useful when we control it instead of allowing it to control our attention. Thank you."

Do not simply stop when you reach the end of your notes.

---

## Know Your Audience

Before speaking, think about:

* Who are they?
* What do they already know?
* What do they need to know?
* Why should they care?
* What language will they understand easily?

A speech for first-year students may need a different vocabulary and level of explanation from a speech for subject experts.

---

## Voice and Delivery

### Pitch

Pitch is how high or low the voice sounds.

* A slightly higher pitch can suit excitement or enthusiasm.
* A lower pitch can suit serious or calm points.

Do not force an artificial voice.

### Volume

Speak loudly enough for everyone to hear.

* Increase volume when necessary for a larger audience or emphasis.
* Lower it when the situation calls for a calmer or more personal tone.
* Avoid shouting.

### Rate

Speaking rate is how quickly you speak.

* Slow down for difficult or important ideas.
* A moderate pace usually helps the audience follow.
* Avoid speaking so fast that words become unclear.

### Pause

A pause can:

* Emphasise an important point.
* Give the audience time to think.
* Separate ideas.
* Give the speaker a moment to breathe or organise the next thought.

---

## Body Language

Useful public-speaking body language includes:

* Suitable eye contact.
* Open posture.
* Natural hand gestures.
* Appropriate facial expressions.
* Standing or sitting steadily.

Avoid constantly looking at the floor, turning your back to the audience, or making distracting repetitive movements.

---

## Language for Public Speaking

Use language that is:

* Clear
* Correct
* Appropriate for the audience
* Easy to follow

Do not use difficult vocabulary only to sound intelligent.

Short sentences can often be easier to deliver and understand.

---

## Handling Nervousness

Feeling nervous before speaking is common.

Useful strategies include:

1. Know your main points.
2. Practise aloud.
3. Take a slow breath before beginning.
4. Start with a prepared opening sentence.
5. Look at friendly or neutral faces in the audience.
6. If you make a small mistake, pause and continue.

A mistake does not require you to stop the entire speech.

---

## Speaking Without Reading

Notes can be useful, but avoid reading every sentence.

Prepare:

**Main idea → 2–3 supporting points → example → conclusion**

You can use keywords rather than full paragraphs.

This usually makes delivery more natural.

---

## If You Forget a Point

Do not panic.

You can:

* Pause briefly.
* Look at your keyword notes.
* Repeat or rephrase the previous idea.
* Move to the next point.
* Continue naturally.

The audience often notices mistakes less than the speaker expects.

## Public Speaking Practice

### Task 1 — One-Minute Speech

Choose one:

* Why communication skills matter
* A skill every college student should learn
* The advantages and disadvantages of social media
* Why students should learn to manage time
* One change I would make in my college

Speak for one minute.

Try to include:

**Opening → 2 points → Conclusion**

### Task 2 — Same Speech, Better Delivery

Give the same speech twice.

**First:** focus mainly on the content.

**Second:** deliberately improve:

* Eye contact
* Rate
* Volume
* Pauses
* Gestures

Notice whether the second version feels clearer.

### Task 3 — Impromptu Speaking

Pick a simple topic at random and speak for 30 seconds without preparing a full script.

Possible topics:

* My favourite food
* A useful app
* Rainy days
* My first day at college
* A person who inspires me

The aim is not perfection. The aim is to practise organising thoughts while speaking.

---

## Quick Checklist

Before speaking, ask:

- Do I know my main message?
- Is my opening interesting and clear?
- Do I have 2–3 main points?
- Am I using suitable language?
- Can everyone hear me?
- Am I speaking too fast?
- Am I using pauses?
- Am I looking at the audience?
- Do I have a clear ending?

## Remember

**Good public speaking is not about sounding perfect. It is about making your message clear, engaging, and appropriate for the audience.**
