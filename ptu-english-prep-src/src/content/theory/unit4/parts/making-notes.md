# Making Notes

## Meaning

Making notes means reducing a passage to its important points in a brief and organised form. Notes are not a full summary: they use headings, subheadings, keywords, abbreviations, and indentation to make information easy to revise.

## Steps

1. Read the passage to understand its main subject.
2. Read again and identify the main points and supporting details.
3. Group related ideas under suitable headings.
4. Use short phrases instead of complete sentences.
5. Create logical numbering and add a key for uncommon abbreviations.
6. Check that the notes cover the whole passage and do not include irrelevant detail.

## Practice passage

Social media has become an important part of teenage life. It helps young people maintain relationships, find information, express creativity, and join communities with shared interests. However, excessive use can reduce sleep, concentration, and face-to-face interaction. Constant comparison with carefully selected online images may also affect self-esteem. Healthy use requires time limits, critical thinking, privacy awareness, and support from family, teachers, and friends.

## Model notes

### Social media and teenagers

1. **Benefits**
   - Maintains relationships
   - Provides information
   - Encourages creativity
   - Builds interest-based communities

2. **Risks of excessive use**
   - Less sleep and concentration
   - Reduced face-to-face contact
   - Lower self-esteem due to comparison

3. **Healthy use**
   - Set time limits
   - Think critically
   - Protect privacy
   - Seek support from trusted people

**Key:** `SM` = social media

## How a passage becomes notes

The original passage contains complete sentences and explanation. Notes remove grammar that is not needed for revision and preserve only the information structure:

```text
Original idea: Social media helps young people maintain relationships and find information.
Notes: Benefits -> relationships; information
```

```text
Original idea: Constant comparison with carefully selected online images may affect self-esteem.
Notes: Risk -> online comparison -> lower self-esteem
```

The arrows show a relationship between ideas; they do not need to be written as full sentences.

## A second example

### Passage

Regular exercise benefits both the body and the mind. It improves strength, flexibility, heart health, and sleep. Exercise may also reduce stress because physical activity can improve mood and confidence. However, people should choose activities suited to their age and health and should increase effort gradually. A balanced routine includes movement, rest, water, and nutritious food.

### Notes

#### Benefits of regular exercise

1. **Physical**
   - Strength and flexibility
   - Better heart health
   - Improved sleep

2. **Mental**
   - Less stress
   - Better mood and confidence

3. **Safe routine**
   - Age/health-appropriate activity
   - Gradual increase in effort
   - Movement + rest + water + nutritious food

**Key:** `PH` = physical; `MH` = mental health

### Common mistakes

- Writing full sentences copied from the passage
- Using too many headings for a short passage
- Leaving out the main idea while keeping minor examples
- Using an abbreviation that is not explained in the key
