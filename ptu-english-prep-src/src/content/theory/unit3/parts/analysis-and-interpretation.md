# Analysis and Interpretation

## What do these skills mean?

**Analysis** breaks a passage into its parts: its ideas, evidence, structure and language. **Interpretation** explains what those parts mean when put together, including a message that may not be stated directly.

Think of analysis as taking a clock apart, and interpretation as explaining how it tells the time.

## Four questions to ask while reading

| Question | What you are looking for |
| --- | --- |
| **What** is the writer saying? | The central idea, argument or theme |
| **How** does the writer support it? | Facts, examples, comparisons, reasons |
| **What is the tone?** | Hopeful, critical, concerned, persuasive, humorous or neutral |
| **So what?** | The conclusion, message or lesson the reader should take away |

## How to write an analytical answer

Use this pattern: **Point, Evidence, Explain.**

1. **Point:** state your answer in one sentence.
2. **Evidence:** support it with a detail from the passage. Quote only a short phrase if needed.
3. **Explain:** say in your own words why the evidence proves your point.

## Practice passage 1: The Heart of Asia

The Heart of Asia conference was created to support the development and security of Afghanistan. Afghanistan is geographically important because it connects Central, South, and East Asia and lies near ancient trading routes. It is also a focus of the region's struggle against terrorism. The passage argues that development cannot be sustainable without peace and that peace requires action against terrorism. At the same time, it warns that closing every channel of engagement with Pakistan may reduce India's and Afghanistan's long-term influence and create new trade difficulties. Regional cooperation is therefore presented as necessary for Afghanistan to become a strong Asian hub.

### Questions

1. What was the initial agenda of the Heart of Asia conference?
2. Why is Afghanistan called a focal point of terrorism?
3. Why are peace and development connected in the passage?
4. What may be insufficient if India and Afghanistan avoid Pakistan?
5. What is the main theme of the passage?

<details>
<summary><strong>Show the model answers</strong></summary>

1. The conference was created to support the development and security of Afghanistan.
2. Afghanistan is a focus of the region's struggle against terrorism, so action against terrorism there matters for the whole region.
3. The passage argues that development cannot be sustainable without peace, and that peace in turn needs action against terrorism.
4. Avoiding Pakistan completely may reduce the long-term influence of India and Afghanistan and create new trade difficulties.
5. The main theme is that regional cooperation is necessary for Afghanistan's security, development and future as a strong Asian hub.

</details>

## Practice passage 2: Why public libraries matter

> A public library is more than a room full of books. For a student who cannot afford many books, it is a free source of knowledge. For a job-seeker, it offers newspapers, computers and a quiet place to prepare. For an elderly person, it can be a friendly place to meet others. Some people argue that the internet has made libraries unnecessary. But not every home has a reliable connection, and not every reader can judge which websites are trustworthy. A library gives equal access to reliable information, regardless of a person's income. A town that closes its library may save a little money, but it takes away an opportunity that many of its citizens have no other way to find.

### Questions

1. What is the writer's central argument?
2. Name two groups of people who benefit from libraries, and how.
3. What argument against libraries does the writer mention, and how does the writer reply to it?
4. What is the writer's tone: neutral, humorous or persuasive? Give a reason.
5. What does the last sentence suggest about closing a library?

<details>
<summary><strong>Show the model answers</strong></summary>

1. The writer argues that public libraries remain valuable because they give everyone free access to knowledge and reliable information.
2. Students who cannot afford many books can read for free, and job-seekers can use newspapers, computers and a quiet place to prepare. (Elderly people, who find a friendly place to meet others, are also acceptable.)
3. Some people say the internet has made libraries unnecessary. The writer replies that not every home has a reliable connection and not every reader can judge which websites to trust.
4. The tone is persuasive, because the writer gives reasons and answers an opposing view to convince the reader that libraries are needed.
5. It suggests that the money saved is small compared with the opportunity lost, which many citizens cannot find anywhere else.

</details>

## Practice passage 3: a poem

For a literary text, also look at **theme**, **imagery** (words that create pictures), **symbol** and **tone**.

> **The Lamp**
>
> Each evening, when the street grows still,<br />
> An old woman lights a lamp on her sill.<br />
> The flame is small, the wind is strong,<br />
> Yet she keeps it burning all night long.<br />
> Strangers hurry past in the rain;<br />
> Her little light guides them home again.<br />
> So a simple kindness, steadily shown,<br />
> Brightens a road that is not our own.

### Questions

1. What is the poem about?
2. What does the lamp stand for?
3. Which words create a picture of difficulty or danger?
4. What is the tone of the poem?
5. What does the poet mean by "a road that is not our own"?

<details>
<summary><strong>Show the model answers</strong></summary>

1. The poem is about an old woman who keeps a small lamp burning at night so that strangers passing in the rain can find their way home.
2. The lamp is a symbol of simple, steady kindness that helps others.
3. "The wind is strong" and "hurry past in the rain" show the difficult conditions the light has to face.
4. The tone is warm, gentle and admiring, as the poet praises quiet kindness.
5. It suggests that kindness helps people beyond our own circle, so the benefit of a good deed reaches strangers as well as our own family.

</details>

## Literary and knowledge texts: what to focus on

| Type of text | Focus on |
| --- | --- |
| **Literary** (story, poem, essay with feeling) | Theme, character, setting, imagery, tone, message |
| **Knowledge** (article, report, argument) | Subject, argument, evidence, cause and effect, conclusion |

In both, quote only brief phrases and explain them in your own words.

## Common mistakes

- Retelling the passage when the question asks you to explain its meaning
- Stating the tone without a reason from the text
- Saying "the writer is good" instead of naming the theme or the argument
- Writing an interpretation that the passage does not support

> **Remember:** Interpretation is not guessing. Every idea must be linked to something in the passage.
