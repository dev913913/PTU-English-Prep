# Translation Practice

## Meaning and purpose

Translation is expressing a message from one language in another without changing its **meaning, tone or purpose**. In PTU questions, understand the complete sentence first. Then write natural English or Hindi/Punjabi, not a word-by-word copy.

> In the exam, write Hindi in the **Devanagari** script (हिंदी) and Punjabi in the **Gurmukhi** script (ਪੰਜਾਬੀ). The Roman spellings below are given only to help you read the examples.

## A practical method

1. Read the complete sentence and find **who** is doing the action.
2. Find the **tense**, the time expression and the link between ideas.
3. Notice words that carry the main meaning, including negatives such as *not* and *never*.
4. Translate the idea into a natural sentence in the target language.
5. Re-read both versions and check tense, number, articles, prepositions and punctuation.

## Hindi to English examples

### Example 1

**Hindi:** स्वच्छता हमारी सामूहिक ज़िम्मेदारी है।  
*(Swachhata hamari samuhik zimmedari hai.)*  
**English:** Cleanliness is our collective responsibility.

*Samuhik* means collective and *zimmedari* means responsibility. We translate the **meaning** of the phrase.

### Example 2

**Hindi:** विद्यार्थियों ने समय पर अपना काम पूरा कर लिया।  
*(Vidyarthiyon ne samay par apna kaam poora kar liya.)*  
**English:** The students completed their work on time.

The action is complete and in the past, so *poora kar liya* becomes **completed**, not *complete*.

## Punjabi to English examples

### Example 3

**Punjabi:** ਸਾਨੂੰ ਪਾਣੀ ਦੀ ਸੰਭਾਲ ਕਰਨੀ ਚਾਹੀਦੀ ਹੈ।  
*(Sanu paani di sambhaal karni chahidi hai.)*  
**English:** We should conserve water.

The natural English is **conserve water**, not a word-for-word version such as "do the protection of water".

### Example 4

**Punjabi:** ਮਿਹਨਤ ਸਫਲਤਾ ਦੀ ਕੁੰਜੀ ਹੈ।  
*(Mehnat safalta di kunji hai.)*  
**English:** Hard work is the key to success.

Common expressions must be translated as complete ideas, not as separate words.

## English to Hindi and Punjabi

### English to Hindi

**English:** We must respect the environment.  
**Hindi:** हमें पर्यावरण का सम्मान करना चाहिए।  
*(Hamein paryavaran ka samman karna chahiye.)*

### English to Punjabi

**English:** The teacher explained the lesson clearly.  
**Punjabi:** ਅਧਿਆਪਕ ਨੇ ਪਾਠ ਸਾਫ਼ ਢੰਗ ਨਾਲ ਸਮਝਾਇਆ।  
*(Adhyapak ne paath saaf dhang naal samjhaya.)*

## Tense pattern at a glance

The same idea changes with the tense. Compare the five forms of one sentence.

| English | Hindi | Punjabi |
| --- | --- | --- |
| He writes a letter. | वह पत्र लिखता है। | ਉਹ ਚਿੱਠੀ ਲਿਖਦਾ ਹੈ। |
| He is writing a letter. | वह पत्र लिख रहा है। | ਉਹ ਚਿੱਠੀ ਲਿਖ ਰਿਹਾ ਹੈ। |
| He wrote a letter. | उसने पत्र लिखा। | ਉਸ ਨੇ ਚਿੱਠੀ ਲਿਖੀ। |
| He has written a letter. | उसने पत्र लिख दिया है। | ਉਸ ਨੇ ਚਿੱਠੀ ਲਿਖ ਦਿੱਤੀ ਹੈ। |
| He will write a letter. | वह पत्र लिखेगा। | ਉਹ ਚਿੱਠੀ ਲਿਖੇਗਾ। |

## Wrong and right translations

| Idea | Wrong (word for word) | Right (natural) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| मेहनत सफलता की कुंजी है। | Hard work is success's key. | Hard work is the key to success. |
| ਸਾਨੂੰ ਪਾਣੀ ਦੀ ਸੰਭਾਲ ਕਰਨੀ ਚਾਹੀਦੀ ਹੈ। | To us water's care should do. | We should conserve water. |
| उसने किताब पढ़ी। | He read book. | He read the book. |

## Common difficulties

- **Word order:** English usually places the verb after the subject. Hindi and Punjabi usually place the verb at the end.
- **Articles:** Hindi and Punjabi do not always have equivalents of *a*, *an* and *the*, so add them correctly in English.
- **Tense:** decide whether the action is happening, finished or expected.
- **Idioms:** translate the meaning of the expression, not each word.
- **False friends:** similar-looking words may not mean the same thing.
