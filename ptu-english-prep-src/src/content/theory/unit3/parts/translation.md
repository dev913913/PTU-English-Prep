# Translation Practice

## Meaning and purpose

Translation is the process of expressing a message from one language in another language without changing its meaning, tone, or purpose. In PTU questions, understand the complete sentence first and then write natural English or Hindi/Punjabi rather than translating each word mechanically.

## A practical method

1. Read the complete sentence and identify who is doing the action.
2. Find the tense, time expression, and relationship between ideas.
3. Identify words that carry the main meaning, including negatives such as *not* and *never*.
4. Translate the idea in a natural sentence in the target language.
5. Re-read both versions and check tense, number, articles, prepositions, and punctuation.

## Hindi to English examples

### Example 1

**Hindi idea:** Swachhata hamari samuhik zimmedari hai.  
**English:** Cleanliness is our collective responsibility.

The phrase is translated by meaning: *samuhik* means collective and *zimmedari* means responsibility.

### Example 2

**Hindi idea:** Vidyarthiyon ne samay par apna kaam poora kar liya.  
**English:** The students completed their work on time.

The completed action is in the past, so *poora kar liya* becomes **completed**, not **complete**.

## Punjabi to English examples

### Example 3

**Punjabi idea:** Sanu paani di sambhaal karni chahidi hai.  
**English:** We should conserve water.

The natural English expression is **conserve water**, not a word-for-word version such as “do the protection of water.”

### Example 4

**Punjabi idea:** Mehnat safalta di kunji hai.  
**English:** Hard work is the key to success.

Common expressions should be translated as complete ideas rather than as separate words.

## English to Hindi and Punjabi

### English to Hindi

**English:** We must respect the environment.  
**Hindi idea:** Hamein paryavaran ka samman karna chahiye.

### English to Punjabi

**English:** The teacher explained the lesson clearly.  
**Punjabi idea:** Adhyapak ne paath nu spasht roop vich samjhaya.

## Common difficulties

- **Word order:** languages arrange subjects, objects, and verbs differently.
- **Articles:** Hindi and Punjabi do not always use equivalents of *a*, *an*, and *the*.
- **Tense:** identify whether the action is happening, completed, or expected.
- **Idioms:** translate the meaning of an expression, not its individual words.
- **False friends:** similar-looking words may not have the same meaning.

## Practice task

Translate these sentences into English:

1. Paryavaran ki raksha karna hamara kartavya hai.
2. Vidyarthiyon ko niyamit roop se pustakalay jana chahiye.
3. Sanu apne buzurgan da satkar karna chahida hai.
4. Mehnat ate imaandari jeevan vich bahut mahatvapooran han.

### Suggested answers

1. Protecting the environment is our duty.
2. Students should visit the library regularly.
3. We should respect our elders.
4. Hard work and honesty are very important in life.

> **Exam Tip:** A correct translation sounds natural in the target language. Accuracy is more important than preserving the original word order.
